This is an offline validation study, not the live index. It reconstructs the Skindex by running the exact shipped code (the same marketOverview the collector runs — no reimplementation) over Steam's own daily aggregates (median price + units sold per day, per case, fetched logged-out from the public listing pages). The live index starts at its 2026-07-25 adoption and is never backfilled; this page exists so the methodology's behavior through real market history is measured, not asserted.

2 · What each protection is worth — variants

Each variant disables exactly one rule; everything else stays as shipped. The gaps are the measured value (or cost) of each layer over twelve years of real history:

  • Seasoning is worth everything. New cases average −1590%/yr (annualized log) in their first three months and −36%/yr through month twelve, turning positive only after the drop pool retires — and they dominate trading volume while they fall. Without the 365-day seasoning the volume-weighted index compounds to zero; this is the commodity-index "contango" problem in skin form, and the seasoning rule is the roll methodology that fixes it.
  • The clamp costs measured right-tail and buys manipulation resistance. Honest single-name pops get clipped at the same ±5% that caps a manipulator's pump — that asymmetry is the price of a settlement-grade index, and it is now a published number rather than a hope (compare the no-clamp variant).
  • Weighting costs little. Volume-weighted vs equal-weight differ by roughly 2%/yr — the honest cost of making index influence proportional to real traded dollars.

5 · What the backtest changed (SMLX-6)

The first reconstruction attempt ground the index to literal zero — and both causes were methodology defects, not data errors. (1) Penny-era quantization: 2013–2016 cases traded at $0.02–$0.10, where a one-cent tick is a −33/−50% "return"; on days with one or two contributors the mover is its own clamp center, so those ticks passed unclamped and ratcheted the level down. (2) Seasoning far too short: 30 days admitted cases eleven months into a measured −36%/yr supply-decay phase, precisely when volume weights concentrate on them. Both are now shipped rules (marks under $0.25 carry no return; ≥3 contributors or the level carries; 365-day seasoning) — neither changes any live published value, and this page is the evidence for why they exist. That is what a backtest is for.

6 · Honest caveats

  • Different marks. The reconstruction uses Steam's own daily medians; the live index marks from the collector's intraday snapshots. Levels will not be bit-identical going forward — the backtest validates the methodology, it does not extend the live series.
  • Today's basket, applied backward. The 42 cases are the current watchlist; cases essentially never delist on Steam, so survivorship bias is minimal, but basket curation history is not simulated.
  • No manipulation in-sample (probably). The reconstruction shows the rules under (mostly) honest history; the manipulation budget prices what the rules resist. Different questions, both published.
