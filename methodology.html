SMLX-6 · Index & Settlement Methodology

Skindex · methodology version SMLX-6 · any rule change bumps the version — a fixing is meaningless without its rulebook. This document describes a measurement, not an offer, solicitation, or operation of any financial instrument. Derivatives referencing game items may be regulated products in most jurisdictions; nothing here is legal advice.

1 · What this is

Three price indices over Counter-Strike 2 items, plus dated settlement fixings computed from them by fixed rules. Every input is a file committed to this repository by an automated collector (GitHub Actions, every 3 hours), so any counterparty can re-derive every published number bit-exactly from public data — the index operator has no discretion and nothing to assert that you cannot check.

2a · Volume weights (case & liquids; art stays equal-weight)

  • Weight = the constituent's median daily dollar volume (price × units sold) over the 60 days ending on the last day of the prior calendar month, normalized across the family. The median (not mean) means a burst of wash volume moves nothing — inflating a weight requires sustaining fake volume for 30+ days, paying venue fees the whole way.
  • Fully lagged, monthly rebalance: weights change only on the first day of a month, from data that ended before that month began — today's trading can never move today's weights, and the weight set for any month is re-derivable from the committed files alone.
  • Cap 10% (effective cap is max(0.10, 1/N) so small baskets stay feasible): excess weight is redistributed pro-rata across uncapped names, iteratively. With the ±0.05 clamp this bounds any single name's daily pull on the index to 0.10 × 0.05 = 0.5%.
  • Fallbacks, both neutral: a name with fewer than 5 volume observations in the window takes the median weight of observed names (absence cannot be gamed in either direction); a month where no name has observations (index inception) is equal-weight.
  • Why volume-weight at all: it ties index influence to real traded dollars. The cheapest way to move the index becomes buying weight, and weight costs volume — the thin-name cheap corner that survived SMLX-3 (nine near-dead cases, trivially washable) no longer exists. Art is never volume-weighted because appraisal marks have no meaningful volume.
  • The current month's weights are published in data/index.json → market.weights and on each manifest item (weight).
  • The clamp center uses these same weights (SMLX-5). The winsorization center is the weight-weighted median of the day's returns, not the plain one. Under SMLX-4's plain median an attacker who held a count majority of thin names could pump them, drag the median to the fake consensus so the pump sat unclamped, and the clamp would then pull honest names toward the fake move — a cheap attack because thin names are cheap. Weighting the center means moving it requires a >50%-weight coalition, i.e. real traded dollars. A single name is capped at 10%, so no one name can ever control the center. Under equal weights the weighted median equals the plain median exactly, so inception months and fallbacks are unchanged.

3 · Settlement fixings

Averaged fixings are the anti-manipulation choice: to move an N-day mean by 1% an attacker must sustain the push for N days, and the winsorization clamp forces it across ≥20% of index weight — which under volume weights means real traded dollars (§5 prices the cheapest such attack). Fixings publish with value null (“accruing”) until the minimum day count exists — a fixing is never backfilled or edited.

New fixings are additive catalog entries under the same methodology id: each accrues forward from its introduction under its own min-day gate and is never backfilled, and adding one changes no existing fixing's canonical form or hash. That is why SETTLE-CASE-90D (added 2026-07-27) did not bump SMLX-6 — a version bump without a computation change on existing fixings would falsely signal a rules change to every hash verifier.

Suitability, stated plainly: the case family has commodity-grade depth and is the only plausible candidate for continuous instruments; the cash ratio is a spread/convertibility mark suited to dated settlement only; the art index is never suitable for settlement (appraisal marks, single-sale jumps).

3a · Constituent changes — seasoning & the inclusion calendar

  • Founding cohort: items whose first recorded mark is on or before 2026-07-25 (the adoption date, set at SMLX-2 and unchanged since) are included from their first day.
  • New listings season for 365 days before becoming eligible (SMLX-6; was 30). The 2013–2026 backtest measured the case lifecycle: annualized log-returns of roughly −1590% in months 0–3 and −36% in months 3–12, flipping positive after year one (+7% in year 2, +41% beyond year 4) as the drop pool retires. That decay is one item's supply schedule, not the market — and because new cases also dominate trading volume, a 30-day seasoning let volume weights concentrate on mid-decay names: a structural short-tilt that compounded to −95% over the reconstruction while an equal-weight basket gained +2000%. At 365 days the tilt is gone. See the backtest.
  • Scheduled inclusion: an eligible item enters on the first day of the next calendar month after its seasoning completes. Because construction is chained, entry is return-neutral: the index level cannot move on inclusion day — there is no arithmetic to front-run, only the newcomer's future returns.
  • Both-days rule: an item contributes a return only when it has marks on both days of a pair and both days are on/after its inclusion date; art marks carry forward between sparse observations (appraisal semantics).
  • Liquids membership (≥5 median sold/day) is evaluated at computation time over trailing data; the published fixing records in data/settlements.jsonl are authoritative and never rewritten if a re-derivation with later data would classify differently.

4 · Corporate actions & patch events

  • Case leaves the active drop pool — no index action; the item keeps trading and its price keeps marking (this event is the market's core supply catalyst, not an anomaly).
  • Item delisted / renamed / made untradable by Valve — the constituent freezes at its last mark and is removed at the next methodology version; history is never rewritten.
  • Structural platform events (e.g., the July 2025 Trade Protection update) — fixings continue mechanically. Consumers of the fixings define their own halt/deferral rules; the methodology's only promise is that the published series is never edited after the fact.
  • Data-source loss (venue closes, blocks, or changes terms) — affected marks go null and carry forward per family rules; replacement sources require a version bump.

5 · Manipulation budget

A published floor estimate of the fee-burn required to move a fixing 1%: to shift one item's daily median an attacker must set the price on ≥50% of that day's prints (wash fraction 0.5), paying venue fees (Steam 15%, cash venues ~12%) on that volume; inventory and price risk are deliberately excluded, so true cost is higher. Two attack models are priced:

  • Uniform — move every constituent ~1%: fee-burn on the whole basket's wash volume.
  • Concentrated (cheapest weight-accumulation) — the honest minimum: the ±0.05 clamp caps any one name's pull at its weight × 0.05, so moving the index 1% requires controlling at least 0.01 / 0.05 = 20% of total index weight; the optimal attacker accumulates that weight at the lowest fee-burn per unit of weight, and the budget prices exactly that greedy basket against the published weights. Because SMLX-4 weights are proportional to real traded dollars, cost scales with weight everywhere — the attack can no longer hide in thin names. This is the headline number on the dashboard — always quote the cheaper attack.
  • Center capture (unbounded control) — a different kind of attack, priced separately (SMLX-5). The clamp center is the weight-weighted median, so an attacker who controls >50% of index weight owns the center and can then move the index arbitrarily, not just 1%. The budget greedily accumulates that half-weight at the lowest fee-burn per unit of weight — this is the price of control, and it is the number an instrument's open-interest cap must respect, because above it the index is no longer a market measurement.

An N-day fixing multiplies either burn by N. Any credible instrument must cap open interest well below the concentrated figure — and its total notional well below the center-capture figure — §5b turns these attack prices into that per-fixing capacity line.

5a · Mark integrity (INTEG-1) — the tamper detector

The fixings above prove the computation; this layer surveils the inputs. Every mark feeding the index is single-venue at its source, so INTEG-1 corroborates each one against independent evidence and publishes divergence flags with every collector run. Lanes:

Book readings (best bid/ask, ±5% depth, total resting units) rotate ~10 items/run into data/book.json, extracted from the listing page's embedded order-book payload.

Flag-only, by design. Flags never remove a mark or reroute the index. Automatic rejection would hand an attacker a cheaper lever: manipulate the thin corroborating venue (Skinport) to force honest Steam marks out of the index and surgically break return pairs. Detection is published; consumers of the fixings define their own halt rules (§4). And because flags change no fixing computation, this layer does not bump the SMLX version — bumping the id without a computation change would falsely signal a rules change to every hash verifier. The integrity layer versions independently (INTEG-1), and its thresholds are published in every integrity record. Sampling is also jittered: scheduled collector runs add 0–10 min of random delay on top of GitHub's own scheduling noise, so the reading instants are not predictable enough to pin a fake price against.

5b · Open interest these fixings can support

§5 prices the attacks; this section converts those prices into a capacity line. With linear payoffs, an attacker who holds the entire opposing side of open interest N earns N×Δ from corrupting the fixing by Δ — so a fixing is safe while C(Δ) > N×Δ for every available attack, i.e. N < C(Δ)/Δ. Three ceilings apply to each fixing:

  • Concentrated bound — the winsorization clamp bounds what a ≥20%-weight coalition can do, so the reference attack is the cheapest 1% push sustained over the fixing window: bound = cost(1% move over the window) / 0.01.
  • Capture bound — center capture is unbounded in Δ, so on its own it caps nothing. The bound assumes a dispute layer — the §6 verification recipe run by counterparties, or an on-chain optimistic challenge window — under which the largest credible print is Δcap = 5%: bound = capture cost over the window / 0.05. This bound exists only under such a layer; without one, total notional must stay below the raw center-capture cost itself.
  • Hedging ceiling — independent of manipulation: a market maker can only lay off risk in the underlying, so workable OI is additionally bounded by ≈ the basket's daily dollar volume no matter what the attack math says.

The published capacity is min(bounds) / κ with κ = 3 as corruption margin — the attack must cost at least three times what it could win. This is a linear-payoff number only: near-the-money binary or digital payoffs make a tiny Δ decisive, so their capacity on these fixings is ≈ zero. The cash-ratio fixing has no capture model (there is no weighted-median center on a two-leg ratio), so its bound comes from its 1% thin-leg cost alone. Every bound divides the published, rounded costs in the settlement record itself, so any counterparty reproduces the capacity line from the same bytes (budget.oiCapacity, assumptions included per entry).

5c · PERPMARK-CASE — experimental mark preview (not a fixing)

PERPMARK-CASE is an experimental preview of a liquidation-grade mark — the smoothing a perpetual venue would mark positions to — published for observation only: it is not a settlement fixing, carries no hash, sits outside the SMLX-6 canonical catalog, and nothing settles on it. Rule: the median of the last ≤5 daily case-index prints, with a max-step guard — an update that would move the mark by more than 2% instead carries the previous mark and publishes guarded:true. The median means one corrupted print cannot move the mark at all, and the step guard stops even a 3-of-5-print corruption at 2% per update — the purpose is exactly that: no single bad print can materially move a mark liquidations might reference. The whole mark path is a pure fold over the published daily series, so it re-derives bit-exactly from data/index.json; the value below is computed in your browser from that file and compared against the collector-published copy when present.

6 · Verification — check the operator

Every fixing publishes a SHA-256 hash over its canonical form ({methodology, name, window, days, values, value}). The button below re-derives the latest fixings in your browser from the committed series in data/index.json and compares hashes against data/settlement.json. From a shell:

node -e "const S=require('./settlement.js'),c=require('crypto');
const m=require('./data/index.json').market;
for(const [n,f] of Object.entries(S.computeAll(m.series)))
  console.log(n, c.createHash('sha256').update(S.canonical(f)).digest('hex'))"

Continuous verification — run a witness. The button above checks one moment; a witness checks every publication, forever, with your credentials instead of the operator's: fork the repository, enable Actions on the fork, and witness.yml re-derives the full index from the committed raw files (using the collector's own assembly code), verifies every fixing hash byte-exactly, and takes its own independent Steam samples against the published marks — every 6 hours. Any divergence fails the workflow: red CI on your fork plus GitHub's automatic failure email is the alarm, entirely outside the operator's control. This repository also witnesses itself each cycle (data/witness.json) — that catches drift and corruption, but self-attestation can never prove operator honesty; only an independently run witness can. Full trust analysis, attack-by-attack: TRUST_ARCHITECTURE.md in the repository.
